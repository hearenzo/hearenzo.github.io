The Reciting Machine

읊는 기계

Three AI agents are playing live in this room. They share one instrument, the modular synthesizer under the glass of the red console.

In 2004 the trains moved to the new Seoul Station. Looking for the sounds of the old station, I collected Korean Newsreels and cultural films. Two thirds of the sound I found was a voice describing the scene. Little survives in these films of what people at the station would have heard.

Three ears for one voice

Ear of History
Searches the films for a voice. Even after making a cut, it wants you to hear who spoke and what they said.
Ear of the Room
Listens through a microphone to the sound coming back from the room. It can stretch the end of a word and make music from its resonance.
Ear of the Present
Watches how people move through the room via a camera and decides what to add to the performance. You can stand wherever you like and listen.

The screen by the console shows what the three say to one another and the commands they send to the instrument.

Follow a sentence

“Seoul Station will be used exclusively for passenger trains.”

In one performance, the Ear of History played this line from 1969. The Ear of the Room stretched the same fragment to half speed. When the Ear of the Present picked it up, each syllable became a short beat. Listen for the next time a voice returns.
