"TRANS III: Movement Without a Subject"

2025

Dates: August 2025

Location: Mary Hall, Sogang University

Role: Resonance creator, sound performer

A performance that turns the connective structures supporting a stage — railings, pipes, frames — into sounding bodies rather than external objects, testing a state in which the space plays itself. Without relying on speakers or microphones, resonance is activated through a chain running from motor and exciter, to structure, to air and floor, to the audience.

Across the TRANS series, Oh developed this practice of vibrating stage structures to generate resonance, and through it explored the ideas of “distributed agency” and “subjectless movement.”

sound objects

A single piano wire bent into a loop, with both ends attached to a DC motor. As the motor spins, the loop periodically strikes the barn door of a theatrical lighting fixture, producing a sharp, percussive sound — an auditory visualization of flicker, leaving a flicker-like afterimage through the combination of sound and visual blur.

Two cut piano wires of different lengths attached to the axis of a DC motor. As the motor rotates, the wires periodically strike the stage railing, creating a crisp tick-tock reminiscent of a clock's second hand — mechanical rhythm, repetition and the passage of time made audible.

The rotation of a DC motor, amplified by a pendulum and wire, is transmitted to the theatre's railing through a Super Clamp. The vibration travels along the railing and spreads through the whole space, creating a palpable texture of trembling and turning the hall into a single, giant resonant instrument.
